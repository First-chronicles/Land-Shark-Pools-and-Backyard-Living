Construction

What Happens During a Concrete Pool Build

A plain-English walk through each stage, from layout to the first swim.

Read More

Most concrete pools follow the same order. The pool is laid out on the ground and the permit is pulled. The hole is excavated, then steel rebar is set to form the pool's frame. Plumbing and electrical lines go in next.

The shell is sprayed in concrete and left to cure. Then come the waterline tile and coping, the deck, and finally the interior finish. The pool is filled and the water is balanced before your first swim.

Every yard is different. When we quote your project, we'll walk you through the stages for your build. You can see each stage on our gallery and before and after.

Decking

Choosing a Pool Deck for Central Valley Summers

How deck color, material, and drainage affect comfort on hot afternoons.

Read More

Summer afternoons in Clovis and Fresno regularly pass 100°F, so the deck matters as much as the water. Light-colored surfaces absorb less heat than dark ones, which is why light concrete and travertine are common around Valley pools.

Texture matters for footing. A broom or textured finish gives better grip when wet. Deck drains carry splash water away from the pool and the house.

Strips of turf between concrete slabs soften the look and break up large areas of hard surface. See examples on our decking and backyard living services.

Design

Why See Your Pool in 3D Before You Build

What a rendering shows that a flat sketch can miss.

Read More

A 3D rendering shows the pool at its real size on your lot. You can see how much deck is left, where the spa sits, and how the pool looks from your back door.

It also helps you plan safety. California law requires new residential pools to include at least two drowning-prevention safety features, such as a fence that isolates the pool (Health and Safety Code 115922). Placing the fence in the rendering shows how it fits with the rest of the yard.

Changes are easy to make on screen. Ask us for a design to see your own yard.
